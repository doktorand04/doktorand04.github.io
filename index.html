About Me

Hi, I'm Aswin, a doctoral student at the Department of Mathematics and Mathematical Statistics and Icelab at Umeå University. My work sits at the intersection of mathematics and evolutionary ecology. If you're curious about the details, you can read more below.

I hail from a small town in Kerala, a beautiful state in the Western Ghats of India. I had an interest in mathematics from a very young age and would routinely venture into the realm of unknown concepts out of curiosity. This curiosity led me to pursue an integrated Bachelor's and Master's degree in Mathematics, with a minor in Chemistry, from the Indian Institute of Science Education and Research (IISER), Thiruvananthapuram. At IISER, I got to experience what it means to be a researcher and decided to apply for doctoral studies both within and outside India. My background in mathematics, combined with a new-found interest in ecology, led me to pursue a PhD in computational science and engineering, focused on mathematical modelling in evolutionary ecology, at Umeå University. I'm currently in my fifth year, expecting to finish around December 2026.

Outside of research, I split my time between solitary pursuits and social ones. On my own, I enjoy reading, photography, hiking, and the occasional solo gym session as there's something meditative about all four. But I'm equally happy in company: cooking for friends, hosting calm get-togethers, game nights, playing sports, or working out with a small group of 1–3 people. Photography is one of those activities I enjoy either way; sometimes I'm out alone chasing light, other times I'm photographing people. If I had to pick, I'd say cooking is where solo and social life meet best for me: I enjoy it just as much by myself as I do with others.

About My Research

My research looks at how ecological and evolutionary processes shape the link between biodiversity and ecosystem functions, using mathematical models and analysis. I work where dynamical systems theory, ecology, and evolutionary biology meet, building mathematical tools to find out how environmental and other outside factors influence ecosystems. I am particularly interested in how populations change over time as they face environmental pressures and interact with other species. I also study how these evolutionary changes can affect ecological systems in return. To explore this two-way relationship between ecology and evolution, I use methods from nonlinear dynamics, statistical mechanics, and differential equations. My goal is to create solid mathematical foundations for understanding things like adaptive diversification, evolutionary branching, and how complex ecological patterns can come from simple evolutionary rules.
